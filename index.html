Origin & Domestication

Originally domesticated in New Guinea; named 'Noble' for its giant thick stalks and rich sweet juice. Spread to India to become the foundation of commercial sugar.

Class 10 Board Exam Concept

This tropical variety requires a maritime climate without winter frost, providing the direct geographic rationale for why sugar mills flourish in Maharashtra and South India.

GEOGRAPHICAL CRITERIA

Ideal Growing Conditions

Sugarcane requires 10 to 18 months of intensive warmth, sunlight, and moisture to mature.

Temperature

Demands a hot and humid climate with steady temperatures between 21°C and 27°C.

Board Tip: Temperatures below 20°C retard growth; severe winter frost destroys seedlings completely.

Annual Rainfall

Requires an annual rainfall of 75 cm to 100 cm evenly distributed across its vegetative cycle.

Board Tip: Dry tracts in Punjab, Haryana, and Western UP rely extensively on canal and tubewell irrigation.

Soil Suitability

Thrives in deep, rich, well-drained loamy, alluvial, or black lava soils that retain moisture without logging.

Board Tip: Heavy manure and chemical fertilizers (N-P-K) are needed as sugarcane exhausts the soil rapidly.

Ripening Sunshine

Clear skies and sunny days with cool nights before harvest are vital for maximum sucrose crystallization.

Board Tip: Excessive late rain before harvest dilutes the juice and drops sugar mill recovery rates.
REGIONAL DYNAMICS

Sugar Industry Shift: North vs South

Core Class 10 Geography Board Topic: Why are sugar mills expanding rapidly into Peninsular South India?

Factor Northern Belt (UP, Bihar, Punjab) Southern Peninsular Belt (Maharashtra, TN, Karnataka)
Climate & Weather Subtropical climate with severe winter frost and extreme summer dry heat (Loo) that hinder cane growth. Equable maritime tropical climate without winter frost; year-round sunshine and humidity.
Sucrose Yield Comparatively lower sucrose percentage due to shorter maturation windows and temperature fluctuations. Significantly higher sucrose concentration leading to superior sugar output per tonne of cane.
Crushing Window Short crushing season of only 4 to 5 months (November to March). Long crushing season lasting 7 to 8 months (October to May/June).
Hectare Yield Moderate stalk yield per hectare (typically 60–70 tonnes/ha). High stalk yield per hectare (exceeding 90–115 tonnes/ha in canal tracts).
Mill Structure Predominantly older, privately owned mills facing logistical delays. Modern sugar mills operated under thriving farmer-run cooperative societies.
CIRCULAR ZERO-WASTE BIO-ECONOMY

Processing & Industrial By-Products

In the modern sugar economy, every single fraction of the sugarcane stalk is utilized.

01

Sett Planting

Mature stalk cuttings (setts) with 2–3 active eye buds are planted in treated furrows.

02

Ratoon Cropping

After harvesting, the underground roots sprout a second (ratoon) crop, saving seed and tillage costs.

03

24-Hour Crushing

Canes must reach mills within 24–48 hours; delays trigger rapid sucrose inversion into glucose.

04

Crystallization

Raw juice is clarified with lime, concentrated in vacuum evaporators, and crystallized into pure sugar.

Molasses & Bioethanol

Dark residual syrup distilled into industrial alcohol and bioethanol for India's 20% petrol blending (E20) program.

Bagasse Cogeneration

Dry fibrous crushed stalk burned in sugar mill boilers for green thermal & electric power, and manufactured into paper packaging.

Press Mud Compost

Organic filter cake containing phosphorus and calcium. Returned to agricultural fields as natural fertilizer and used for industrial wax.

Jaggery (Gur) & Khandsari

Traditional unrefined artisanal sweeteners rich in minerals (iron, magnesium), powering widespread cottage employment in rural India.
